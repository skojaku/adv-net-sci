% Break the page unless #1 of it is left. This is the one macro this sheet
% wants out of needspace.sty, written out because needspace is in
% texlive-latex-extra and not in a BasicTeX install, which is what the sheets
% get built on by hand. \pagegoal is \maxdimen on an empty page, so the test
% is false there and a heading at the top of a page never breaks again.
\newcommand{\needroom}[1]{%
  \par
  \begingroup
    \setlength{\dimen0}{#1}%
    \dimen2=\pagegoal \advance\dimen2 by -\pagetotal
    \ifdim \dimen0>\dimen2 \newpage \fi
  \endgroup}

% A part heading. It carries the break test, because a heading printed at the
% foot of a page with nothing under it is worse than a short page.
\newcommand{\parthead}[2]{%
  \needroom{8\baselineskip}%
  \partheadhere{#1}{#2}}

% The same heading with no break test, for the one that is already inside a
% minipage -- \pagegoal and \newpage mean nothing in there.
\newcommand{\partheadhere}[2]{%
  \vspace{0.5em}
  {\large\bfseries Part #1 --- #2}\par
  \vspace{-0.4em}\rule{\textwidth}{0.8pt}\par
  \vspace{0.1em}}

\parthead{1}{A letter from Omaha}

In 1967 a man in Omaha, Nebraska was handed a letter addressed to a stockbroker
outside Boston --- a stranger, thirteen hundred miles away. He could not post
it. He could pass it only to somebody he knew on first-name terms, who passed
it on the same way.

{\bf Question 1}: The letter arrived. How many hands did it pass through on the
way? Write your guess, and write why you guessed that.

\say

\parthead{2}{Ringville}

Sixteen people live in Ringville and they sit in one circle. Everybody is
friends with the two on their left and the two on their right: four friends
each, thirty-two friendships in the town.

\begin{center}
\resizebox{0.46\textwidth}{!}{\ringtown{}{\dfill{1}{0}\dfill{2}{1}}}
\end{center}

{\bf Question 2(a)}: Person 1 has a letter for person 9. Trace the chain on the
drawing. How many handshakes does it take? \blank

{\bf Question 2(b)}: Now everybody: in each person's box, write how many
handshakes it takes to reach them from person 1. Two are filled in, and you may
find you only have to work out half of the rest.

{\bf Question 2(c)}: Add up every number in the boxes: \blank\ Share that
total out among the fifteen other people --- the town's \textbf{average}:
\blank\ And the biggest number in any box --- the \textbf{worst case}: \blank

\needroom{5\baselineskip}%
{\bf Question 2(d)}: One of those two numbers you would give to a newspaper.
The other you would give to an engineer who has to promise the letter arrives.
Which is which, and why?

\say

{\bf Question 3}: Ringville grows. A thousand people, still one circle, still
four friends each. How many handshakes from somebody to the person sitting
directly opposite them? \blank\ And if the circle held all eight billion people
on Earth? \blank\ So could a world wired like Ringville get the Omaha letter to
Boston in six handshakes, and why?

\say

\parthead{3}{Two shortcuts}

Two of the sixteen went away to college and each came home with one friend from
the far side of town: person 1 is now also friends with person 9, and person 5
with person 13. Nothing else changed --- thirty-four friendships, not
thirty-two.

\begin{center}
\resizebox{0.46\textwidth}{!}{\ringtown{\shortcuts}{\dfill{1}{0}\dfill{9}{1}}}
\end{center}

{\bf Question 4}: Fill in the boxes again, for the town with the two new
friendships. Two are done. Then the same three numbers: new total \blank\ new
average \blank\ new worst case \blank

\needroom{5\baselineskip}%
{\bf Question 5}: Thirty-two friendships became thirty-four. Work out both
changes as percentages --- friendships went \emph{up} by \blank\% and the
average went \emph{down} by \blank\%. Say, in your own words, what those two
lines did that thirty-two ordinary friendships could not.

\say

Three people who are all friends with each other make a \textbf{triangle}.
Ringville, before the two shortcuts, has sixteen of them.

{\bf Question 7(a)}: Then the two shortcuts went in. Do persons 1 and 9 have a
friend in common? Do 5 and 13? So how many triangles now? \blank

{\bf Question 7(b)}: The real model does not \emph{add} a shortcut, it
\textbf{moves} one: person 1 drops person 2 and befriends person 9 instead.
Which friends did persons 1 and 2 have in common? \blank\ So how many triangles
does the town lose? \blank
