{\LARGE\bfseries Module 5 --- Pitch your partition}\hfill{\color{softgrey}\small 30 minutes, in groups}

\vspace{0.2em}
\rule{\textwidth}{0.8pt}

\textbf{The situation.} The lecturer has drawn where everyone in this room sits. Each person
is a node, and two people who sit close are joined by an edge. That is a network of about
15 nodes. Nobody knows which communities it ``really'' has: there is no answer key. Your
group makes one partition of it and has three minutes to say why that partition is the one
to use. You may argue only from your own definition of a community.

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\bfseries}c >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.7cm} p{12.2cm}@{}}
1 & Shape, by eye &
A community is a set of nodes that hold together. Find, in the picture, (i) the $k$-core
with the largest $k$ (everyone has at least $k$ neighbours inside the set), (ii) the largest
clique (everyone is joined to everyone), (iii) the largest pseudo-clique with density
$\rho\ge 0.8$ ($\rho$ = edges inside / possible edges inside). Give its members the number 1
and everyone else 0. Work out $k$, the size and $\rho$ by hand from $A$ and the arrays. \\
2 & Cut, by eye &
A community is one side of a split with few edges between the sides, relative to size.
Make exactly two groups so that
Ratio cut $=\sum_c \ell_c/|V_c|$ and Normalized cut $=\sum_c \ell_c/\text{vol}(V_c)$
are as small as possible, where $\ell_c$ is the number of edges leaving group $c$ and
$\text{vol}(V_c)$ is the sum of its members' degrees. A plausible split is not enough:
compute both values by hand for at least three candidates and keep the smallest. \\
3 & Chance: modularity, igraph &
A community is a set with more edges inside than a random network with the same degrees
would put there. Maximize
$Q=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{ij}\bigl[A_{ij}-\frac{k_ik_j}{2m}\bigr]\delta(c_i,c_j)$.
Type the code from your sheet. \\
4 & Pattern: block model, graph-tool &
A community is a set of nodes that connect to every group in the same way. The grouping
that gives the shortest description of the network wins. Type the code from your sheet. \\
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{The graph is a choice too.} Where people sit is not a network. The notebook joins
each person to their \texttt{K} nearest people. \texttt{K} is a variable: change it and
the network, and every answer, changes. Say which \texttt{K} you used when you pitch.

\medskip
{\large\bfseries Plan}\par
\vspace{-0.3em}\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\bfseries}r p{1.9cm} p{12.6cm}@{}}
10 min & Prepare &
Make your partition. Then write four sentences: (a) your definition of a community;
(b) who your partition puts together, and who it separates from whom; (c) one number
that supports it; (d) one thing it cannot do. The other groups will find (d) if you do
not. \\
12 min & Pitch &
Three minutes per group, in the order 1, 2, 3, 4. Everyone else writes down one question
for the group that is speaking. \\
5 min & Questions &
Each group asks its question. The answer must come from the answering group's own
definition. Before you call another group's partition wrong, say whose definition you are
using. \\
3 min & Discuss &
Which partition is correct? What would you need to know to say? \\
\end{tabular}

\medskip
{\large\bfseries Questions to ask}\par
\vspace{-0.3em}\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\begin{itemize}
\item What did you decide before you started (\texttt{K}, the number of groups, $k$, $\rho$, the objective)? What changes if you decide differently?
\item Pick a node that sits between two groups. Why is it with the group it is in?
\item Does your method need to be told how many groups there are?
\item Which of the four would you use to form discussion groups for this class? Does the answer depend on what the groups are for?
\end{itemize}
