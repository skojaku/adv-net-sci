\textbf{How to use this sheet.} Each framed \textbf{Box} below goes into the
matching empty cell of the notebook. \textbf{Type it by hand} rather than pasting it:
writing a line is how you find out what it does. Press \textbf{Shift+Enter} to run
a cell. The notes under a box say what each numbered line does.

\textbf{Typing tips.} Spelling, capital letters, brackets and quotation marks all
matter. Colab may add a closing bracket or quote for you; when you type the closing
one yourself, it steps over the extra one. Indent with the Tab key.

\parthead{1}{Meet the data}

Zachary's karate club: 34 members of a university karate club, studied in the
1970s. Two members are linked when they were friends outside class. During the
study the club split in two after a quarrel between the instructor, \textbf{Mr.\ Hi}
(node 0), and the administrator, \textbf{the Officer} (node 33). We know who went
with whom, which makes this network the standard test case for community detection.

\parthead{5}{Draw the groups on a circle}

Another way to look at a grouping: every member on a circle, each group on its own
arc. This box uses the drawing tools themselves, \texttt{matplotlib} and igraph,
instead of the ready-made \texttt{show}.

\input{boxes/box5}

\textbf{What to look at.} Which friendships run from one arc to another? Compare the
two circles: where do they put the same members, and where do they differ?
