\question{2}{6}{The likelihood of the degree-corrected block model.}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=pltblue!4, colframe=pltblue!35, boxrule=0.5pt,
                  sharp corners, boxsep=2pt, left=6pt, right=6pt,
                  top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
{\small
An undirected network has $N$ nodes. It may have several edges between two
nodes, and self-loops. For $i \neq j$, $A_{ij} = A_{ji}$ is the number of edges
between $i$ and $j$; $A_{ii}$ is \emph{twice} the number of self-loops at $i$,
because a self-loop has both its ends at $i$. The degree is
$k_i = \sum_j A_{ij}$. Node $i$ belongs to group $c_i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$.
The \emph{degree-corrected block model} draws, all independently,
\[
  A_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\!\left(\theta_i \theta_j\, w_{c_i c_j}\right)
  \ \text{ for every pair } i < j,
  \qquad
  \frac{A_{ii}}{2} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\!\left(\tfrac12\, \theta_i^2\, w_{c_i c_i}\right)
  \ \text{ for every node } i,
\]
where $\theta_i > 0$, $w_{rs} = w_{sr} > 0$, and
$\sum_{i:\,c_i = r} \theta_i = 1$ for every group $r$. Write $m_{rs}$ for the
number of edge ends in group $r$ whose other end is in group $s$ (an edge
inside $r$, and a self-loop in $r$, count twice in $m_{rr}$), and
$\kappa_r = \sum_{i:\,c_i = r} k_i$ for the total degree of group $r$. Drop
any term that depends on none of $c$, $\theta$, $w$.

\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{Careful with the self-loops.} Keep them, exactly as defined: a
self-loop counts 2 in $A_{ii}$ and in $k_i$, and the \emph{number} of
self-loops is the Poisson variable, with mean $\tfrac12 \theta_i^2 w_{c_ic_i}$.
Drop them, or treat $A_{ii}$ like any other pair, and the sums in (a) no
longer close.
}
\end{tcolorbox}

(a) [2 pts] Write the log-likelihood
$\mathcal{L}(c, \theta, w) = \log P(A \mid c, \theta, w)$, self-loops
included. Simplify it until no sum over pairs of nodes is left.

(b) [2 pts] Fix the assignment $c$. Find the maximum-likelihood estimates
$\hat{\theta}_i$ and $\hat{w}_{rs}$.

(c) [2 pts] Substitute them back, and write $\mathcal{L}$ as a function of the
assignment $c$ alone, in terms of $m_{rs}$ and $\kappa_r$.

\vfill

{\footnotesize\color{rulegrey}
Done early? (not graded) Without the $\theta$'s, the same steps give
$\tfrac12 \sum_{r,s} m_{rs} \log\!\big(m_{rs} / n_r n_s\big)$, where $n_r$ is
the number of nodes in group $r$. What did $\theta$ change, and why does that
stop the model from putting all the hubs in one group?}

{\large\bfseries Hints for Question 2}\par
\vspace{-0.4em}{\color{rulegrey}\rule{\textwidth}{0.6pt}}\par
\vspace{0.2em}
{\small Read only as far as you need. Each hint names a move; working it out is
still yours.}

\vspace{0.6em}

\textbf{Hint 1 --- The Poisson distribution, and the self-loops.}
If $X \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$, then
$P(X = x) = \lambda^{x} e^{-\lambda} / x!$. Everything is independent, so the
likelihood is a product: one factor for each pair $i < j$, and one for each
node $i$, its self-loops. The logarithm turns the product into sums. At a
self-loop the Poisson variable is $A_{ii}/2$ and its mean is
$\tfrac12 \theta_i^2 w_{c_ic_i}$, not $A_{ii}$ and $\theta_i^2 w_{c_ic_i}$.
That convention makes each self-loop term exactly half of what the pair term
would be at $j = i$, up to a constant. Then, for any symmetric $F_{ij}$,
\[
  \sum_{i<j} F_{ij} \;+\; \frac12 \sum_i F_{ii}
  \;=\; \frac12 \sum_i \sum_j F_{ij} .
\]

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Hint 2 --- Pair terms and node terms.}
Inside $\sum_i \sum_j$, $A_{ij}$ is a \emph{pair} term: it depends on both $i$
and $j$. $\theta_i$ is a \emph{node} term: it depends on $i$ alone. In the
inner sum over $j$, a node term of $i$ never changes, so it comes out as a
coefficient:
\[
  \sum_i \sum_j A_{ij}\, f(\theta_i) \;=\; \sum_i f(\theta_i) \Big( \sum_j A_{ij} \Big) .
\]
What is the bracket? Since $A_{ij} = A_{ji}$, a node term of $j$ comes out of
the sum over $i$ the same way.

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Hint 3 --- One block at a time.}

\vspace{0.2em}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\hspace{0.5cm}}c@{\hspace{0.5cm}}c@{\hspace{0.5cm}}c@{\hspace{0.5cm}}c}
\matpanel{0} & \raisebox{1.8cm}{$\Rightarrow$} &
\matpanel{1} & \raisebox{1.8cm}{$\Rightarrow$} &
\matpanel{2} \\[0.2em]
\parbox[t]{3.9cm}{\centering\footnotesize the adjacency matrix, nodes ordered
  by group; $A_{66} = 2$ is one self-loop} & &
\parbox[t]{3.9cm}{\centering\footnotesize cut into blocks, one for each pair
  of groups $(r, s)$} & &
\parbox[t]{3.9cm}{\centering\footnotesize one block, $(r, s) = (2, 2)$: every
  entry in it has $c_i = 2$ and $c_j = 2$}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Order the nodes by group and the adjacency matrix falls into $K \times K$
blocks. Inside the framed block every entry has $c_i = r$ and $c_j = s$, so
$w_{c_ic_j} = w_{rs}$ is a constant there: it comes out of the block's sum,
just as a node term did in Hint 2. So sum one block at a time,
\[
  \sum_i \sum_j \;=\; \sum_r \sum_s \ \sum_{i:\,c_i = r} \ \sum_{j:\,c_j = s} .
\]
What does the sum of the entries in the framed block count? Do the same with
the term that has no $A_{ij}$ in it, and then use the normalization of
$\theta$.

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Hint 4 --- Lagrange multipliers.}
A Lagrange multiplier turns a maximization \emph{with} a constraint into one
\emph{without}. To maximize $f(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ subject to $g(x) = 0$, add a
new variable $\mu$ and maximize, with no constraint,
\[
  F(x, \mu) \;=\; f(x) - \mu\, g(x) :
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i} = 0 \ \text{ for every } i,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial F}{\partial \mu} = 0 \ \ (\text{which is } g(x) = 0 \text{ again}).
\]
With several constraints $g_r(x) = 0$, use one multiplier for each:
$F = f - \sum_r \mu_r\, g_r$. Here there is one constraint per group,
$\sum_{i:\,c_i = r} \theta_i - 1 = 0$.
