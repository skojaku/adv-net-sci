% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
A social network has $N = 10{,}000$ people and $M = 13{,}114$ friendships.
$n_k$ is the number of people with exactly $k$ friends. Nobody has a degree
that is not in the table.

\begin{center}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{|c||*{10}{C{1.05cm}|}}
\hline
$k$   & 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 & 16 & 32 & 64 & 128 & 256 & 512 \\ \hline
$n_k$ & 5000 & 3232 & 1143 & 404 & 143 & 50 & 18 & 7 & 2 & 1 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\question{1}{5}{The people.}

The CCDF of the degree is $P(k)$, the fraction of nodes with degree $> k$.

(a) [2 pts] Let the degree be continuous with density $p(k) = C k^{-\gamma}$
for $k \ge 1$, where $\gamma > 1$. Compute the CCDF $P(k)$. What is the slope
of $\log P(k)$ against $\log k$?

(b) [1 pt] Plot the CCDF of the network in the table on log--log axes. Draw
the axes by hand: space the powers of ten evenly, and use the same length
for one decade on both axes.

(c) [2 pts] Read the slope of your line and give $\gamma$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\question{2}{5}{The friends.}

List every (person, friend) pair in the network; each friendship gives two.
A \emph{friend} is the second entry of a pair drawn uniformly at random.

(a) [2 pts] Plot the CCDF of a friend's degree on log--log axes, with the
Question 1 points on the same axes for comparison.

(b) [3 pts] Read the slope of the friend line. By how much does it differ
from the slope in Question 1, and why?
