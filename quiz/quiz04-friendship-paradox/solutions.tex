\question{1}{5}{The people.}

(a) [2] $C = \gamma-1$, so $P(k) = \int_k^\infty (\gamma-1)k'^{-\gamma}dk' = \ans{k^{-(\gamma-1)}}$:
a line of \ans{slope $1-\gamma$}. Marking: integral set up $+1$; result and slope $+1$.
Skipping $C$ is full marks. Slope $-\gamma$: $0$ of the second point.

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.56\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
(b) [1] Nine points on a line: people $>k$ (strictly), divided by $N$. Hand-drawn
log--log axes in any base with evenly spaced powers; linear axes $0$. The
$k=512$ row is $0$; no penalty for omitting it. $P(K\ge k)$ (shifted a step): $-0.5$.

(c) [2] Slope $\ans{\approx -1.5}$ (accept $-1.3$ to $-1.7$), so
$\ans{\gamma = 1-\text{slope} \approx 2.5}$ (accept $2.3$ to $2.7$); e.g.\ $k=2$ to $128$:
$-2.77/1.81 = -1.53$. Two-point slopes are fine. Marking: slope $+1$; $\gamma$
from it $+1$. $\gamma=1.5$ (slope read as $-\gamma$): 1 of 2.

\question{2}{5}{The friends.}

(a) [2] A random (person, friend) pair hits a friend of degree $k$ with
probability $k\,n_k/2M$. Weight each row by \ans{$k\,n_k$}, count down from the
bottom, divide by $2M$. Misplaced point: $-0.5$ each. Unweighted: $0$ of 2. Weighted by
$k^2$, or divided by $N$: 1 of 2. The last three points bend down; that is correct.

(b) [3] Slope $\ans{\approx -0.55}$ (accept $-0.4$ to $-0.8$; a whole-line fit gives $-0.65$),
\ans{shallower by about 1}, since $q(k) = k\,p(k)/\langle k\rangle$ has CCDF slope
$2-\gamma = -0.5$: each person is counted once per friendship. Marking: slope $+1$;
``shallower by about 1'' $+1$; reason $+1$. ``Friends have more friends'' with no link to the
slope: $+0.5$ of the last point. At $k=64$: 0.1\% of people but 7.3\% of friends.
