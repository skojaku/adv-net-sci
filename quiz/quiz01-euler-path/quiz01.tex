\question{1}{4}{Even degrees are not enough.}

The graph here is undirected. The \textbf{degree} of a node is the number of
edges touching it. An \textbf{Euler path} is a walk that uses every edge
exactly once.

Here is a tempting rule: \emph{if every node has even degree, an Euler path
exists.} It is false.

\vspace{0.2em}

\textbf{(a) [3 pts]} Give a counterexample. Your graph must have at most
6 nodes, an even degree at every node, at least one edge at every node, and no
Euler path. Name every node and give its degree.

\vspace{0.2em}

\textbf{(b) [1 pt]} The tempting rule is missing one requirement. Name it, and
say in one sentence why your graph violates it.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\question{2}{6}{Now the edges have directions.}

Every edge now carries an arrow, and a walk may only follow the arrow. An
Euler path in a directed graph is a walk that uses every directed edge exactly
once.

\vspace{0.2em}

\textbf{(a) [3 pts]} Write the condition under which a directed graph has an
Euler path. Use the words \emph{in-degree} and \emph{out-degree}, and do not
forget the condition that has nothing to do with degrees.

\vspace{0.2em}

\textbf{(b) [2 pts]} Give a directed graph with 4 or 5 nodes that \textbf{has}
an Euler path. Name every node, give every edge its direction, and say where
the walk starts and where it ends.

\vspace{0.2em}

\textbf{(c) [1 pt]} Give a directed graph with 4 or 5 nodes that has
\textbf{no} Euler path. Then name the node or nodes that break your condition,
and give their in-degree and out-degree.
