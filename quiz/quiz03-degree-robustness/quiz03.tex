{\small
Notation.
$G$ is a finite, simple, undirected network with $N$ nodes and $M \ge 1$ edges.
Node $i$ has degree $k_i$ and neighbor set $\mathcal{N}(i)$. The degree
distribution $p(k)$ is the fraction of nodes of degree exactly $k$, and
\[
  \langle k \rangle = \sum_k k\, p(k) ,
  \quad
  \langle k^2 \rangle = \sum_k k^2 p(k) ,
  \quad
  \mathrm{Var}(k) = \langle k^2 \rangle - \langle k \rangle^2 ,
  \quad
  \kappa \;\equiv\; \frac{\langle k^2 \rangle}{\langle k \rangle} .
\]
}
\end{tcolorbox}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\question{1}{5}{Your friends have more friends than you.}

The average degree of a friend is the friend's degree averaged over
every (person, friend) pair in $G$ --- that is, over all $2M$ edge endpoints:
\[
  \langle k_f \rangle
  \;\equiv\; \frac{1}{2M} \sum_{i} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} k_j .
\]

(a) [3 pts] Prove that $\langle k \rangle \le \langle k_f \rangle$ in
every such network.

\vspace{0.1em}

(b) [2 pts] State exactly when equality holds, and prove it.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\question{2}{5}{How much can you delete before it falls apart?}

Let $G$ be a configuration model network: $N$ nodes are given degrees
from $p(k)$, each is cut into that many half-edges, and the half-edges are
paired uniformly at random. Assume $N$ is large and the network is
locally tree-like. A giant component holds a non-vanishing
fraction of the $N$ nodes as $N \to \infty$.

Now delete every node independently with probability $f$.

\vspace{0.2em}

(a) [3 pts] Derive the critical fraction $f_c$ at which the
giant component disappears. Give $f_c$ in terms of $\kappa$.

\vspace{0.1em}

(b) [2 pts] Evaluate $f_c$ for (i) a Poisson $p(k)$ of mean
$\langle k \rangle$, and (ii) $p(k) \sim k^{-\gamma}$ with
$2 < \gamma < 3$ as $N \to \infty$. Say in one sentence what (ii) means for
such a network.
